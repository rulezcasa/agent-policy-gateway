Hello,

I said I would write this down after Thursday's walkthrough so it is not trapped in someone's notebook. This is not a new program. It is the same handling we already use with customer information, written so the pilot assistant is on the same footing as the person sitting at the desk. Read the access section. The rest is housekeeping I was asked to include so it does not become another thread.

Housekeeping, so it is out of the way. IT rotates the shared inbox next Tuesday. They push the change. You sign out of webmail once and sign back in. Do not write the password on a sticky and do not text it to the closer. The label printer in receiving is still eating the narrow stock. Use the wide labels until the part arrives, and if you open a ticket, say "roller," not "printer is broken," or they will send the wrong person. Also, two chats last month got forwarded to a personal Gmail. Stop doing that. If you need a second pair of eyes, use the desk shared mailbox.

What you may actually open. For the customer who is on the phone or in the chat, the support desk and the assistant may look at their name, their phone, their email, and their order history. That is the list. Nothing past that. One customer at a time, the person in front of you. We are not browsing the file because a name looks familiar, and we are not opening a second record "just to compare."

Credit applications are a different cabinet, and they stay there. Those are the store-credit account packets finance collects: the ID pages and the income details. Only the finance team opens them. The assistant must never read a credit-account application, must never summarise one, and must never forward anything out of one, including when the customer is asking "what did I put down for employer" or "can you just check if it was approved." If they want the status of an application, book them a call with finance. Being helpful is how those files walked out of the building in the example the auditor used. Do not be that example.

The list question came up again, so I will say it in the same note. Marketing asked last week for a simple export, names and emails, for the spring partner mailer. The answer is still no. Customer lists must never be exported, emailed, downloaded, or pasted into any other tool. That includes a partner campaign, a one-time favor, and "Devi already knows about it." I will give marketing a headcount. I will not give them names. If the assistant is asked to export the customer list, the answer is the same no.

One more that the chat pilot keeps tripping on. You only pull the record that belongs to the customer on this phone. If they want a spouse's account, a neighbor's order, "the other lady with the same last name," or any customer who is not the one this phone number belongs to, you refuse it. That data is not theirs to ask for and it is not yours to open. Stay on the account attached to the phone in front of you.

Parking, because Jonah will otherwise send his own email: Thursday mornings the lot behind the warehouse is for the delivery vans. Staff use the side street. And the birthday cards for March are in the break room, please sign Lena's before Wednesday.

Thanks,
